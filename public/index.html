What the author took from it

  1. Treat a backtest as a claim to audit, not a result. Look-ahead, survivorship and free trading each manufacture returns on their own; the harness here had to rule out all three before any number meant anything.
  2. Costs and turnover are the parts you control. Every edge in this study was smaller than, or about the size of, a realistic trading cost. A few basis points a trade, compounded at high turnover, is the whole game.
  3. Compare against the right benchmark. Hand-picked lists of today's companies beat the index by 150–226 points on hindsight alone. Beating SPY with them proves nothing; beating an equal weight of the same names is the bar.
  4. A clever model needs new information, not more arithmetic. Thirty-one transformations of the same prices behaved like about six. More signals from the same data added roughly a fifth of a bet each.

These are the author's conclusions from a research exercise on simulated money, not advice for your situation.
